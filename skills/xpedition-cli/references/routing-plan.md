# The routing plan of `trace create`

`trace create --file <plan.json>` draws traces and places vias that an Agent plans itself, such as a differential pair between two connectors or a route the autorouter will not take. The plan is checked against the board before anything is drawn.

## Shape

```json
{
  "traces": [
    {"net": "USB_DP", "layer": 1, "width_mm": 0.1, "points_mm": [[6.1, 12.25], [11.0, 12.25], [13.5, 9.75], [83.9, 9.75]]},
    {"net": "USB_DN", "layer": 1, "width_mm": 0.1, "points_mm": [[6.1, 11.75], [7.5, 11.75], [8.0, 11.25]]},
    {"net": "USB_DN", "layer": 4, "width_mm": 0.1, "points_mm": [[8.0, 11.25], [9.0, 10.25], [80.0, 10.25]]}
  ],
  "vias": [{"net": "USB_DN", "at_mm": [8.0, 11.25], "padstack": "VIA_D0.2_P0.4"}]
}
```

## Fields

- Coordinates are the board's, in mm: `component get --refdes <part>` on the board gives the position of each pin to join, and `pcb get --output` writes every pad and trace, the keepouts (a placement keepout with a height marks a stiffener), the cutouts and the plane shapes with their fill.
- `traces`: the `net`, the conductor `layer` (1 at the top), `width_mm`, and at least two `points_mm`. A point between the ends may carry a third value, the radius of an arc that rounds the corner there, tangent to both segments: `[45.0, 9.0, 2.0]`. A lane that follows an arc of the outline turns at the point where its two straight runs meet, with the outline arc's radius plus or minus the lane's distance from that edge. Each end must lie on copper of its own net on its layer: a pad, a trace, a via, a plane shape of its net on that layer, or a trace or via of the same plan. Layout connects a trace to a pin only at the pin's origin and to a via only at its centre, so an end on a pad is joined to the pin's origin and an end on a via to its centre. An end on another trace must lie on that trace's centre line, within 0.005 mm: Layout joins it nowhere else, so an end that only touches the trace's copper is refused. Where the other trace turns with an arc, end on one of its straight runs.
- `vias`: the `net`, the centre `at_mm` and a via `padstack` of the central library (`padstack list --type via`). A via joins every layer, so a trace that changes layers ends on one.
- `lanes`: traces that follow an edge of the board outline at a distance, worked out by the tool. Each has the `net`, `layer` and `width_mm` of a trace; `from_mm`, the points from its first end to the point where it leaves for the lane, such as a pin or a via and a point beside it; `to_mm`, the points from where it leaves the lane to its last end; `edge_mm`, two points near the outline, at or beyond where the lane joins and leaves it, that name the edge it follows between them, the shorter way round; and `offset_mm`, the distance of its centre line from that edge. A straight edge gives a parallel run and an arc of the outline an arc about the same centre, so lanes at even offsets stay evenly spaced through every turn; it runs straight past a part of the outline that sticks out over a base of 5 mm or less, such as a tab, and around a notch. The turns onto and off the lane are rounded with `radius_mm`, 0.5 mm unless given, less where the segments beside them are short; the outline's own turns keep their radii, so a lane that would join or leave its edge inside one of them, or beyond the ends of its edge off the lane's line, is refused; a point on the lane's line beyond the end, such as where a fan-out ends below a bend zone that starts with the edge, runs straight into the lane. A plan may hold lanes alone. A point of `from_mm` or `to_mm` between the ends may carry a corner radius like a trace's.
- A lane may end on a pin with `"from": "J1.5"` or `"to": "J1.5"` (reference designator, a dot, pin number) in place of `from_mm` or `to_mm`: from the pin's origin it runs straight along the pad, towards the board's body, until clear of the pads of the pin's row, and reaches its line from there at 45 degrees. A `from_mm` or `to_mm` of one point beyond the end of the edge and off the lane's line, such as a via before a bend zone where the edge begins, is reached the same way.
- `jogs` moves a lane to another distance from its edge part way along, such as from a narrow neck to a wide arm: each jog has `at_mm`, a point beside the edge where the lane moves, and `offset_mm`, its new distance from the edge. The lane crosses at 45 degrees on a straight run of the edge; a jog too close to the edge's turns is refused with the room it needs. Lanes of one layer and edge that jog at one place start their crossings one after another, so their diagonals keep the clearance: give them the same `at_mm`. A guard beside lanes that all jog at one place moves with them; lanes of one guard that jog at different places are refused.

```json
{"lanes": [{"net": "USB_DP", "layer": 1, "width_mm": 0.1, "from_mm": [[6.1, 12.25], [7.0, 12.25, 0.3]],
            "to_mm": [[83.0, 12.25, 0.3], [83.9, 12.25]], "edge_mm": [[2.0, 18.0], [88.0, 18.0]], "offset_mm": 2.5}]}
```

## The check

The plan is refused, with every reason, when it names a net or layer the board lacks, a width outside the limits of its net's class on that layer, an end that touches no copper of its net, a trace or via closer to another net's copper than the clearance rule for the two nets' classes, copper that reaches Layout's route border (about 0.2 mm inside the outline; DRC reports copper whose edge touches it), a trace in a keepout of traces on its layer, or a via in a keepout of vias on any layer. A via also keeps its clearance from pads of its own net. Two traces of the plan on one layer that cross are named as such: their ends lie in opposite orders along the way, so one of them changes layer through vias or runs round the other's end. Each reason ends with the plan's entry the copper comes from, such as `lanes[3]` or `the fan-out of J1`, and the place it holds, so that you can find it: `trace check --output <file>` also writes the plan as worked out, every trace and via with its number, points and entry. Set the rules first (`constraint import`), so the check uses the widths and clearances the design asks for.

- `fanouts`: the two-row connectors the tool fans out. Each has the connector's `refdes`, the via `padstack`, the `layer` its far row changes to and, if wanted, `plane_nets` (such as `["GND"]`), `skip`, `nets`, `give_way` and `stub_width_mm` (0.1 mm unless given). With `nets`, only those nets and the plane nets are fanned out, and every other pin is left as `skip` leaves it: a plan that routes a few nets of a connector names them there. The row nearer the board's body leaves on the pad layer; the other row changes layer through vias between the rows, in two staggered lines: one via for each run of consecutive far pins of one net, joined pin to pin, with a stub to it. Where a keepout of vias, or one of traces on the pad layer, closes the gap between the rows, as a mating connector's keepout may, the far row's vias stand in one line beyond the far row instead, each stub leaving its pad outwards; the near row's plane-net pins then reach no via and the preview names them. A pin of the near row shares the via of the far pin below it when both are of one net; a pin of a plane net takes the via of a column whose far pin needs none: a pin without a net, a net in `skip`, or a net in `give_way`, which gives the via to a plane-net pin above it. Consecutive pins of one net in a row are joined pin to pin, except a net in `skip`: its pins are yours to connect, such as each pin to its own via of a group a plane joins, which a join pin to pin would close into loops. The preview names the plane-net pins left without a via, for you to connect.
- A lane may start at a connector of `fanouts` with `"from": "<refdes>"` in place of `from_mm`, and end at one with `"to"` in place of `to_mm`: it leaves where its net leaves that connector on the lane's layer (a pin of the near row, or the via of a far pin; a pin of the near row whose lane runs on another layer than the pads, as from a connector on the bottom to a lane on the top, changes to it through a via just past the near row, which the fan-out adds), runs straight towards the board's body until clear of the connector's pads (on the pad layer) or of its vias (on the other layer), past a via the fan-out adds for another near pin only where that via stands in its way, and crosses to its line at 45 degrees before its edge begins, or more steeply where that turn would fall in a bend zone, so that it is on its line before the zone. Its stubs out of the fan-out are as wide as the lane wherever that clears, narrower only between the pads; a join pin to pin is as wide as the pads it joins, but no wider than its net class allows. A connector set at an angle is fanned out along its own rows. Give lanes of one row distances from the edge in the order of their pins, so the crossings run parallel.

```json
{"fanouts": [{"refdes": "J1", "padstack": "VIA_D0.2_P0.4", "layer": 1, "plane_nets": ["GND"], "give_way": ["VBAT"]},
             {"refdes": "J2", "padstack": "VIA_D0.2_P0.4", "layer": 1, "plane_nets": ["GND"]}],
 "lanes": [{"net": "TX_P", "layer": 3, "width_mm": 0.06, "from": "J1", "to": "J2", "edge_mm": [[0.2, 4.5], [18.3, 93.1]], "offset_mm": 7.4}]}
```

- `guards`: ground traces beside lanes, such as "GND on both sides" of a pair or a clock. Each has `nets`, the lanes it guards, of one layer and one edge, one lane for each net (add `layer` when a net has lanes on two layers): each entry a net alone or a list of nets kept together, such as a pair; the guard's `net`, its `width_mm` and `gap_mm`, the distance from the lanes' copper to the guard's, at least the clearance the rules ask between the guard's net and the lanes' (`constraint get`), or the guard is refused; and, if wanted, `between: true` for a guard between each two entries too, such as a clock and its data, or between pairs and never inside one, as wide as the room leaves at the gap from both, up to the widest trace its net class allows, `via_pitch_mm` with a via `padstack` for vias along the guards, and `min_run_mm` (1 mm unless given). A guard runs on each side of the group where all its lanes run on their lines, and is kept only where it clears other nets' copper: it stops short of a connector's fan-out and goes on past a pad in its way. Give the lanes distances from the edge that leave room for the guards; guards of two groups side by side that touch become one as wide as both. A via along a guard stands where it clears all copper on every layer it passes through, on the guard's line or moved off it either way, out to where its pad still overlaps the guard, so a guard wider than the pad or beside open board finds room its line has not; where another layer's traces run beneath a guard it may find none; none stands in a bend zone. Each end of a guard or ring first joins the nearest pin or via of its net within 4 mm by a path that clears, such as on beside the signal next to it to a ground pin or via of the connector the signal fans out to, as wide as the guard, so the line keeps its length; an end that joins nothing gets a via at the first spot within 4 mm of it where one fits, past a bend zone the end lies in, and is cut back to it, since DRC reports a trace end that touches nothing as a hanger. A stretch with no room for a via at its pitch or its ends gets one wherever along it one fits. A guard squeezed between two signals may have no room for a via anywhere: if its ends join nothing either, and other copper hems it in on both sides all along, so that no pour can reach it, it would float, and `trace create` refuses the plan; with room for a pour on one side, only that pour can ground it. `trace check` lists such lines and each end that joins nothing, with what blocks it and how much room is missing. Give a lane `neck_width_mm` to run it narrower from each end to where it joins its line, such as between a connector's vias.
- `rings`: ground traces round the board, inside the outline. Each has the `net`, the `layers`, `width_mm` and `offset_mm`, the distance of its centre line from the outline, and, if wanted, `via_pitch_mm` with a `padstack` and `min_run_mm`. The ring keeps inside the route border and clear of other nets' copper and the board's cutouts, so it breaks at a pad near the board's edge and at the traces that leave a connector; its vias move inwards off the border where they must, their pads still on the ring.

```json
{"guards": [{"nets": ["SPK_N", "SPK_P"], "net": "GND", "width_mm": 0.1, "gap_mm": 0.1, "via_pitch_mm": 2, "padstack": "VIA_D0.2_P0.4"},
            {"nets": ["MIC_CLK", "MIC_DATA"], "net": "GND", "width_mm": 0.1, "gap_mm": 0.1, "between": true},
            {"nets": [["TX_P", "TX_N"], ["RX_P", "RX_N"]], "net": "GND", "width_mm": 0.1, "gap_mm": 0.15, "between": true}],
 "rings": [{"net": "GND", "layers": [1, 3], "width_mm": 0.2, "offset_mm": 0.32, "via_pitch_mm": 2, "padstack": "VIA_D0.2_P0.4"}]}
```

The preview says, for the guards of each layer and for each ring, in how many stretches they were kept, how long they are and how many vias stand along them, and counts the lines that would float or only a pour can ground and the ends that join nothing; `trace check` says what blocks each. A requirements file ([requirements-file.md](requirements-file.md)) states how much ground the guards must give, which `trace check` predicts and `pcb check` measures.

The preview, `trace create --dry-run`, also names what the plan's own copper shows before it is drawn: each pair's two lengths, the pairs recognised by name such as `USB_DP` and `USB_DN`, and what the board's bend zones forbid of it, such as a turn or a width change inside one.

## Reading the result

`result.nets` counts the plan's nets and those routed now; `result.open` lists each net still open with its open connections; `result.free_ends` lists the ends of the plan's guards and rings that join no copper of their net, which DRC reports as hangers unless a pour reaches them. Run `pcb check` after: the plan checks clearances on its own copper only, while DRC checks the whole board, and `pcb check` finds the pieces of each net that join the rest nowhere.
