# Route a flex board between two connectors

A flex board that joins two boards carries one bus between two board-to-board connectors. Read the knowledge entries `layout-flex-bend-and-stiffener` and `layout-flex-connector-bus` first, and the team's flex and routing rules that `xpedition-cli knowledge list --source team --applies-to trace create` returns: they decide the fan-out, the clearances, the via limits and where vias may go.

1. Read the board. `pcb get` gives the outline, the stiffeners (`keepout_kinds.height_limits`), the bend zones (`bend_zones`, each with what marks it), the plane shapes (`planes`), the cutouts, the net classes and the via padstacks; `pcb get --output <file>` adds every pad, keepout and cutout shape. `component get --refdes <connector>` gives each pin's net and position, which shows the two rows and the pins that match from one end to the other. When the board marks no bend zone, ask the person where it bends. A bend zone that runs at a slant across the board carries its shape in `polygon_mm`, and the checks use that shape.
   - When a structure drawing marks where a connector goes, with placement keepouts in the shape of its pads, `component move --refdes <connector> --to-marks` moves it over them. Check `marks.pins` (the pins that landed on a mark) and `marks.off_mm` in the result, and read `marks.other_places` when the drawing marks more than one place for it.
2. Set the rules. Give the power and speaker nets classes as wide as their current needs, define the differential pairs and the spacing between the two nets of a pair, then `constraint import --file <rules.json>` ([rules-file.md](rules-file.md)).
3. Plan the lanes. Write one routing plan for `trace create` ([routing-plan.md](routing-plan.md)):
   - Give every net a lane under `lanes`: its `offset_mm` from one board edge, the edge named by two points of `edge_mm`, and its fan-out at each end in `from_mm` and `to_mm`. The tool follows the outline with it, so lanes at offsets one pitch apart (width plus clearance) stay evenly spaced through every turn, and a pair's two lanes come out nearly the same length.
   - List each connector under `fanouts`, with the ground net in `plane_nets` and a power net whose pins are many in `give_way`: the tool puts the far row's vias between the rows and gives each net its way out. Start each lane at the connector with `"from"` and end it at the other with `"to"`; the tool turns it onto its line before the bend. A guard or a ground lane that ends on one pin names it, `"to": "J2.19"`. A lane that runs in a narrow neck and a wider arm moves out with `jogs` where the board widens. Draw yourself only what the fan-out leaves: pins at the connector's ends outside its two rows, a power net's copper, and the plane-net pins the preview names. Otherwise fan the traces out from the pins to their lanes inside the stiffener: the row nearer the board's body on the pad layer, the other row through one via beside each pin. A lane on another layer than a connector's pads, such as a top-layer lane at a connector on the bottom, changes layer through a via the fan-out adds just past the near row; list only the nets the plan routes in the fan-out's `nets`. A via takes a padstack the design uses (`pcb get` `via_padstacks`) or one of the central library (`padstack list --doc <project> --type via`); `padstack create` adds one. Finish every turn and width change before a bend zone, and cross the zone straight. On a board whose necks and arms are bend zones from end to end, with no stiffener, finish the turns at the connectors and run the lanes parallel to the edges through the zones: the turns the outline's own bends force are then the only ones `pcb check` lists as `bend_turn`; report them as the outline's.
   - Give each ground pin a via beside it inside the stiffener.
   - When the rules ask for ground beside signals, list the groups under `guards`: each pair, a speaker or bias pair, a clock with `between` to keep it apart from its data; leave each group's neighbours the room of its guards. When they ask for ground round the board, add a ring under `rings` on the outer layers. Give both `via_pitch_mm` and the via padstack the rules name.
   - A power net on many pins that reaches another layer through several vias joined there by a copper area: list it in the fan-out's `skip` and join each pin to its nearest via alone. Joined pin to pin as well, its copper closes a loop through every pair of vias.
   - Write the reviewer's list as a requirements file ([requirements-file.md](requirements-file.md)): ground beside which nets, the ring, the via padstack, the via pitch, the pours.
   - Run `trace check --file <plan.json> --requirements <requirements.json>`. It works the plan out as `trace create` would, draws nothing, and lists what `trace create` would refuse (`blocked`), each guard or ring line that would float (`floating`), each that only a pour can ground (`pour_only`), each end that joins no copper of its net (`free_ends`), each with what blocks it, the clearance left against the clearance needed and the next step, and the requirements as the plan would meet them. Its notes give each pair's two lengths and what the bend zones forbid. Change the plan until nothing is blocked or floating, and the requirements fail only where the result says there is no room.
4. Draw. `trace create --file <plan.json>` refuses a plan that breaks a width or a clearance, or whose guard or ring line would float; change the plan and run it again. Its result says for each net whether it is routed, and lists the ends left joined to nothing.
5. Pour. Pour a power net's copper areas first (below), then `plane create --net GND --layer <n> --bends --hatch diagonal --hatch-width <w> --hatch-distance <d>` for each layer: solid with a hatched band over each bend zone, cut around the power copper; a zone at a slant is hatched by its own shape, the heads beside it left solid, and tied square to the sides it shares with the solid copper, or along the board's edges where nothing fits so; a hatched piece no tie reaches stays without copper, which `pcb check` lists as `plane_without_copper`; for a layer without a bend zone, or one band alone, use `--from-y` and `--to-y`. A power net carried on two layers gets a copper area at each end on each layer, `plane create --net <power> --layer <n> --polygon <points>` over its pins and lanes inside the stiffener, joined through the layers by vias inside it (`via create --net <power> --x --y`), as many as its current needs. A copper area gets copper only once a pad, trace or via of its net touches it on its layer: read `generated` in `pcb get` `planes`.
6. Stitch. `via create --net GND --pitch <distance>` places a grid of ground stitching vias wherever the ground copper of two layers meets, none in a bend zone or a keepout of vias; a grid point without room moves to the nearest free spot within half the pitch; `--from-y` and `--to-y` (or `--from-x` and `--to-x`) keep it to a part of the board, and `via create --net GND --x --y` places one.
7. Open and mark. When the rules or the reviewer ask for bare ground at the outline's ears or behind the stiffeners, follow the knowledge entry `layout-solder-mask-openings`: for each ear in `pcb get` `ears`, `solder-mask create --side <side> --net <ground net> --polygon '{"points_mm": <its points_mm>}'`; for each stiffener in `keepout_kinds.height_limits`, `solder-mask create --side <its side> --net <ground net> --fit` with its extents as the rectangle. Put the board's name on the silkscreen with `silkscreen-text create --text <name> --x --y --rotation 90deg` between the bend zones (knowledge entry `layout-silkscreen-text`).
8. Check. `pcb check --requirements <requirements.json>` lists the hazards and the board rules (on a flex board what each bend zone and stiffener forbids, and `pair_skew` for a pair whose two lengths differ by more than 0.127 mm), each net in more than one piece with its islands, and the requirements as measured. An island that holds no pin floats: its `tie` is a path, on a layer, that joins it to the rest of its net; add it to the routing plan as a trace of that net and draw it, or delete the island. A plane-net via that other nets' vias close in on every layer gets no pour and shows here. `pcb get` must report no open net; its `metrics.pairs` gives each pair's two lengths, the pairs the design defines (by name only when it defines none), and its `metrics.widths` each net's narrowest and widest trace. A guard requirement counts the ground pour beside a trace as well as ground traces. To take back a mistake: `trace delete --nets <net> --from-x --to-x --from-y --to-y` deletes only the net's traces wholly inside the rectangle, such as a stub that ends on nothing, and `doc undo` takes back this tool's last write on the board, one write per call. Report each requirement as measured, each pair's length difference against the interface's limit, each power net's width and via count against its current, and any rule you could not meet with the reason the results give.
