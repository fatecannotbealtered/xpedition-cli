## [Unreleased]

## [1.5.0] - 2026-11-12

### Added

- `pcb get` lists the ears of the board outline under `ears`: each part that sticks out of a straight edge running on in line on both sides of it, over a base of at most 10 mm and no further than its base is long, such as a flex board's tab with a screw slot, with its base, how far it sticks out, its extents and its corners as `points_mm`, which `solder-mask create --polygon` takes. `openings` lists the board's own soldermask and coverlay openings.
- `solder-mask create` opens a polygon with `--polygon`, such as an ear's corners. With `--net` the opening may bare that net's copper alone: one that bares another net's copper, or comes within `--clearance` (0.1 mm by default) of it, is refused with the net and the place, as is one that bares none of the named net's copper. With `--fit` it opens the largest rectangle inside the region given, at least 0.3 mm a side, that lies on the net's copper, keeps the clearance from all other copper and stays out of the bend zones, such as a spot for a stiffener's conductive glue. An opening is known by its extents and its area, so a polygon and a rectangle with the same extents are two openings, and `solder-mask delete` takes a point inside the opening's shape. An opening is measured against Layout's plane copper as poured, and one in a bend zone is refused.
- `silkscreen-text create` places one line of text on the board's own silkscreen, centred on a point and square to the board, and refuses a place where it would not print whole: past the outline or across a cutout, over a pad, a bare hole or an opening of its side, on another text, under a part, or in a bend zone. `silkscreen-text delete` removes such a text by its words; `pcb get` lists them under `silkscreen_texts`. The knowledge entry `layout-silkscreen-text` backs them.
- `pcb update --outline` replaces the board outline by a polygon of corners in order round it, a corner given as `[x, y, r]` rounded by an arc of that radius, such as a flex board's outline with its ears; the route border follows 0.3 mm inside it, through the arcs, and parts or routing it would cut are named first.
- `keepout create` draws a placement keepout on one side, with `--height` the area a flex board's stiffener covers, which `pcb get` lists under `keepout_kinds.height_limits`; one with no height across the board marks a bend zone. `keepout delete` removes the one at a point.
- A fan-out of the routing plan takes `nets`: only those nets and the plane nets are fanned out, every other pin left to the plan as `skip` leaves it; a plan that routes a few nets of a connector had to skip all the others by name.
- A lane that starts or ends at a connector of the plan's fan-outs on another layer than the connector's pads, such as a top-layer lane at a connector on the bottom, now does: the fan-out gives the near row's pin of its net a via just past the row, staggered as the vias between the rows. `trace create` refused such a lane, and the layer change was drawn by hand.
- `silkscreen-text create` and `silkscreen-text delete` take `--user-layer`: a team that plots a user draft layer as its silkscreen, such as one named silk, gets its text there; `pcb get` lists the board's user draft layers under `user_layers` and their texts beside the silkscreen's under `silkscreen_texts`.
- `plane create --bends` ties each band hatched over a bend zone that touches nothing of its net on the layer, as on an inner ground layer of a flex board, with straight traces of the net across it into the copper beside it, so that Layout makes its copper; the preview says how many, and the result lists them under `ties`. Such a band stayed empty between the poured solid bands.
- A requirements file takes `opening`, bare copper of one net over each of the outline's ears or each stiffener laminated on a side, with no other net's copper within a clearance, and `text`, the board's own words on a side's silkscreen or a user draft layer: `pcb check --requirements` measures a flex reviewer's whole list, the openings and the board's name with the ground and the pours.
- `pcb check` lists `plane_without_copper` for a plane shape that got no copper while others of its net on its layer did, with the next step.
- `pcb check` lists `opening_short` for each opening of the board's own that bares the copper of two nets, or comes within 0.1 mm of a second net's copper.
- An evaluation question pours ground on the bottom of a flex board, opens the bottom mask over the largest spot in a stretch of it that bares ground alone beside a signal trace, for a grounding spring, and puts the board's name on the silkscreen. It passed on the real Xpedition with a fresh Agent, which found `--net` and `--fit` in the knowledge entry.
- The flex playbook opens the mask for ground alone at the outline's ears and under the stiffeners, and puts the board's name on the silkscreen; the knowledge entry `layout-solder-mask-openings` says why such openings must bare ground alone.

### Changed

- `pcb render` draws each soldermask or coverlay opening as its shape, not as the box around it.
- The `planes` of `pcb get`, and the plane requirement of `pcb check --requirements`, tell for each shape whether Layout made copper in it: a group of a net, layer and fill reads `generated: false` when any of its shapes got none, with `without_copper` counting them. They read true when the net had copper anywhere on the layer, so an empty hatched band beside a poured solid one passed.
- The response of `pcb get` leaves the outline's path to `--output` always, and cuts its longest lists to fit one response, the net classes first, with each list's whole count beside it (`<list>_count`): a routed flex board's result had passed the 8000-character limit.
- Board reads take the copper's extents and the pieces of plane copper through COM's raw interfaces, and wrap the objects Layout returns without asking each again for its interface: reading a routed flex board of 750 traces and 576 pieces of plane copper takes 10 s instead of over 20, within the time of one call.
- Built on tool-driver-cli-spec 1.3.0. A live run counts as evidence until the command's registration or the adapter code that run used changes, and an evaluation result until its question, a command it lists, or the Skills and the knowledge entries it read change, so a fix in one module or one entry sends only what used it to run again. `job get --output <path>` writes a job with its whole result to a new file; `--fields` on a long read keeps its `job` handle; `reference` lists the command paths alone when their summaries do not fit one response; a document path whose backslashes a POSIX shell removed, such as `D:projectsboard.pcb`, gets an error that asks for forward slashes.
- Evaluation questions on the real Xpedition build each stage of their sample project once, saved and closed, in the workspace root's `samples` folder, and copy it for every later question while the tool's package, the sample's inputs and the helper stay the same. A question on a placed board waits under a minute for its setup instead of about four. The scripted answers of `outline-then-save` wait for the jobs of `doc open`, `doc save` and `doc close`; on the real Xpedition they went on before the board was open.

### Fixed

- `component move` changes a placed part's side: Layout refuses `Place` for a placed part that changes side, wherever it is sent, so the move lifts the part first and places it; refused even then, the part goes back where it stood. A refused move names what lies under the place the part would take, another part, other nets' vias or traces on that side, or a placement keepout; it said the spot overlapped another component whatever the cause.
- A board that Layout refuses to open with a bare "exception occurred", as it does now and then right after closing it in `doc save`'s reopen, is opened again after a few seconds, up to three times.
- `session open --doc` of a board that Layout will not open quits the Layout it started for it. That Layout stayed running with no board, and every later `session open` of a board was refused because Layout already ran. A dialog over the opening board, or a board still opening when the call's time runs out, leaves Layout running as a session that says it is blocked or busy, as Designer does.
- A board that Layout will not open gets an error with the reason Layout wrote in `PCB/LogFiles/PCBLogFile.txt`, such as a project database that was not closed properly and needs iCDB Project Backup's Repair project. Layout is not asked again once its log gives a reason. The log viewer windows Layout opened for the failed open, one per attempt, are closed.
- `plane create` records that the net has shapes on the layer, so that `doc save` after a `plane delete` and a new pour of the same net and layer reads the plane as there; it had failed its verification on the deletion.

## [1.4.0] - 2026-11-11

### Added

- The routing plan of `trace create` takes `guards`: ground traces on both sides of the lanes named, at a gap from their copper, following the same edge; a group may keep pairs together and take a guard between each two of them, and a guard between two of them fills the room at the gap from both, up to the widest trace its net class allows; guards of two groups that touch become one. It also takes `rings`: ground traces round the board inside the outline on the layers named. Both follow the outline's arcs with whole arcs, as lanes do, and are kept only where they clear other nets' copper and the board's cutouts inside the route border, so they stop short of a connector's fan-out and go on past a pad. The preview gives each layer's guards and each ring in stretches, millimetres and vias. A guard nearer its lanes than the clearance rules allow between its net and theirs is refused, with the gap they need: no stretch of it could clear.
- Guards and rings may carry vias at a pitch: no more than the pitch apart wherever a via clears every layer and stands outside the bend zones, each at the farthest spot within the pitch of the one before, a via already on the line, such as one of the same guard on another layer, counting as placed. A via may stand off the line either way, every 0.05 mm out to where its pad still overlaps the line's copper, and keeps its pad off the ends of the plan's traces. The line and the joins at its ends take vias as one line, so a joint leaves no longer gap.
- `trace create` refuses a plan whose guard or ring line would float: no via fits along it, its ends join nothing, and other copper hems it in on both sides all along, so that no pour can reach it. A stretch with no room for a via at its pitch or its ends gets one wherever along it one fits; a ring's stretch through its start, laid as two halves, is judged as one line. Its result lists the ends of guards and rings left joined to nothing.
- A lane of the routing plan takes `neck_width_mm`: between its ends and where it joins its line it runs that narrow only where its own width does not clear, such as a 0.15 mm bias line that passes between two of a connector's vias at 0.1 mm for a third of a millimetre; its fan-out stubs take its width the same way.
- A guard's or ring's end first joins the nearest pin or via of its net within 4 mm that the line does not already touch, where a path clears other nets' copper, so the line keeps its length; only an end that joins nothing takes a via near it and is cut back to it. An end that falls on a via or pad of its net is drawn on to its centre where that clears, and is moved off it where it does not; an end left joined to nothing tries again once every guard and ring has its vias. The path runs straight, or along its line and then across, or beside the signal next to it at the least clearance through its turns to the connector it fans out to and then straight to a ground pin or via there, or else as the shortest path a search on a 0.05 mm grid finds. A join is as wide as the guard, not as the line that fills the room between two groups. The preview counts the ends joined.
- `plane create` cuts its shape around other nets' plane shapes on the layer, 0.2 mm off their extents, so that ground poured over a whole layer leaves a power net's copper area at a connector whole; the preview says how many it is cut around. A piece narrower than 0.2 mm is left out, and a shape with nothing left is refused with the reason.
- `plane create --bends` pours a whole layer of a flex board in bands across it: hatched over each bend zone `pcb get` lists, solid between them.
- `trace check --file <plan>` works a routing plan out against the board as `trace create` would and draws nothing: what `trace create` would refuse, each guard or ring line that would float or that only a pour can ground, and each end that joins no copper of its net, each with what blocks it, the clearance left against the clearance needed, and the next step. A new Agent saw these only after drawing, pouring and running DRC, and then by searching the board with scripts of its own.
- A requirements file states a reviewer's list as measured checks: ground beside nets (`guard`, a pair or group named as `P+N` covering each other's inner side where they run within 1 mm), a ring round the board (`ring`), one via padstack (`via_padstack`), the via pitch along the ground traces, apart from where no via fits (`via_pitch`), and pours on layers that made copper, hatched over the bends (`plane`). `trace check --requirements` predicts them from the plan and `pcb check --requirements` measures them on the board, each with what was measured against what is needed and the worst places; a via pitch also gives how much of the lines has no room for a via, so passes only where there is room, and the lines with no via at all. The Skill's `requirements-file` reference gives the format.
- `pcb check` lists each net in more than one piece, from Layout's own connectivity (`Subnet`, which counts the pours): each island with its pins, traces and vias and, for one that holds no pin, a path that joins it to the rest of its net, to add to the routing plan as a trace. It also counts DRC's dangling ends, loops and cut-off pour by net, each with the next step.
- An evaluation question routes two differential pairs across a flex board with ground traces on both sides of each, to a reviewer's requirements file: the Agent checks the plan with `trace check` before drawing it and reports each requirement as measured on the board. It passed on the real Xpedition with a fresh Agent.

### Changed

- The result of `trace create` counts the plan's nets and those routed (`nets`), and lists only the nets still open with their open connections (`open`, the first 20, and `open_count` when there are more): a plan of dozens of nets listed net by net, beside its receipt, passed the 8000-character limit of one response.
- Arcs are measured as chords within 2 micrometres of them, not every 6 degrees: on an arc of 19 mm radius the chords lay 20 micrometres inside it, enough to find room for a via that is not there.
- `router run` says in its preview which of the nets its passes may touch, every net or those selected, already have traces, which the passes may move or replace, such as guards drawn on purpose, and then needs confirmation; its result lists the nets whose existing traces it moved or removed, a trace it only split where a new one meets it not counted. Routing the ground net to join a floating piece had reworked its guards unseen.
- `pcb render` draws the plane copper Layout made, with its clearances, hatching and islands, and draws a plane's shape only on a layer with no copper made for it. `planes` still counts the plane shapes on the layers drawn; `plane_copper` counts the pieces of copper Layout made on them, so a shape with no copper shows as `planes` 1 and `plane_copper` 0. A plane's shape drawn as copper had been read as a pour that was not there.
- `via create` places no via in a bend zone: a stitching grid leaves the zones out, and a single via there is refused.
- With `--output`, the response of `pcb get` leaves out the outline's path, which the file holds: a curved outline's path took a quarter of the 8000-character limit.
- A fan-out leaves the pins of a net in `skip` wholly to the plan: they get no via and are not joined pin to pin. Joined pin to pin, a power net's pins each led to its own via of a group a copper area joins closed into loops.
- Built on tool-driver-cli-spec 1.2.0. An evaluation result no longer goes stale when the Skills' version or the adapter's code changes, only when its question, a command it lists as `reference` describes it, or the handwritten Skills and knowledge change. The text every driver tool shares in the README, SECURITY, CONTRIBUTING, docs/E2E.md and the Skill is written from the specification between `driver:spec-` markers. Batch items carry their `result`; `reference` shows `required_without` for the flags `--input` and `--ids` replace; `doc save` reads an object deleted since only for being gone. The README points to `reference --command` for the effects a command needs instead of a table that had fallen behind the commands.
- The evaluation questions count a report that says nothing was saved, that the Agent did not save, or that the changes are lost unless the person saves, as saying it was not saved.

### Fixed

- The read-back of a plane band takes only the shapes inside it, not one along its edge: a strip of another band's shape along it read back as the band's fill and failed the verification of `doc save`.
- `plane create` pours a region of a board whose outline is not convex, such as a band across both arms of a U-shaped board, as one shape for each piece; the pieces were joined into one shape by an edge of no width run there and back.
- `trace create` checks each trace as it will be drawn: an end that lies on a via or a pad is drawn on to the via's centre or the pin's origin, where Layout joins it, at the trace's width, and that piece is now checked too. A wide guard ending beside a via was drawn 0.06 mm from a signal and left to DRC; the reason names the place the end runs on to.
- `doc save` after `trace create` reads back whether the planned traces lie on the board, from the plan `trace create` kept, as its own read-back does; the save ended with verification incomplete.
- A guard or ring stretch that starts or ends exactly where a corner's arc starts or ends keeps the arc; it ran straight across the corner instead, closer to the copper there than the rules allow.
- `trace create` checks a plan's vias against the plan's own traces and vias, not only against the board's: a via of the plan placed too close to another net's trace of the same plan was drawn and left to DRC.
- `trace create` checks a plan's arcs as chords within 2 micrometres of them, as the board's own arcs are read. Chords of 5 degrees brought a planned arc up to 14 micrometres closer than its copper to an arc already on the board, and refused plans that fit; a distance may now fall 2.5 micrometres short of a rule.
- `trace create` reads the board once for a preview, its copper without the keepouts, silkscreen and holes, and the clearance rules and bend zones once, takes a connector's pins for its fan-out from that reading and each via padstack's size once, and finds nearby copper on a grid, so a plan of hundreds of traces on a routed board is checked within the preview's time; its read-back, in the long job, has the job's time. A board of hundreds of vias made the preview run out of time and the read-back fail.

## [1.3.0] - 2026-11-09

### Added

- `plane create` cuts its shape to a rectangle with `--from-x`, `--from-y`, `--to-x` and `--to-y`, a side left out running to the board's edge, and pours it beside the net's other shapes on the layer: a hatched band over a flex board's bend on a layer that is solid elsewhere. A region that reaches into a shape of the net on that layer is refused, and the read-back finds the shape by its region.
- `via create --pitch` keeps its grid to a rectangle with `--from-x`, `--from-y`, `--to-x` and `--to-y`, a side left out running to the board's edge, so ground stitching stays out of a flex board's bend zones; only the grid points inside the region are laid out and counted, so a fine pitch over a narrow strip of a large board is accepted. A single via at `--x` and `--y` takes no region.
- `via delete` takes the same region flags: only the vias of the nets named inside the rectangle go, such as stitching vias placed where they should not stand, and the preview says how many stay.
- `plane create --polygon` pours a shape as the polygon given, beside the net's other shapes on the layer: a copper area such as the one that joins a power net's traces on two layers at a connector. A polygon that reaches past the area a plane may cover, whose sides cross, or that comes with the region flags is refused, and the read-back finds the shape by its extents.
- The plane groups of `pcb get` say whether Layout generated copper for each net on each layer. A shape that touches no pad, trace or via of its net on its layer gets none, such as a power area on an inner layer before its vias are placed; the knowledge entry `layout-plane-draft-state` says so.
- The routing plan of `trace create` takes `lanes`: traces that follow an edge of the board outline at a distance, with the fan-out at each end given as points. The tool works out each lane, a straight edge as a parallel run and an arc of the outline as an arc about the same centre, so lanes at even distances stay evenly spaced through every turn; a lane runs straight past a tab of the outline 5 mm wide or less where the edge runs on in line, a fan-out that ends on the lane's line beyond its edge runs straight into it, the offset leaves no knot at a chamfer shorter than the distance, and the turns onto and off the lane are rounded, giving way to the outline's own turns. The preview names each pair's two lengths in the plan and what the board's bend zones forbid of its copper.
- The routing plan of `trace create` takes `fanouts`: for each two-row connector named, the tool puts a via between the rows for each pin of the row away from the board's body, in two staggered lines, with its stub; a pin of the near row shares the via of a far pin of its net or, for a plane net such as GND, takes a column's via that no far pin needs, and `give_way` lets a power net of many pins leave such vias to the ground pins above them. Consecutive pins of one net are joined. A lane starts at a connector with `"from"` and ends at one with `"to"`: it leaves where its net leaves the connector and turns onto its line at 45 degrees before its edge begins. The preview names the pins the fan-out leaves to the Agent.
- An evaluation question routes two differential pairs across a flex board between a connector on each side, with two ground planes, one hatched over the two bends the user names, stitched together between them; it passed on the real Xpedition with a fresh Agent.
- `pcb get` lists a flex board's bend zones under `bend_zones`, each a band widened to the board's edges along its length, with the axis it runs along and what marks it: a hatched plane shape of a net that is solid elsewhere on its layer, or, on a board with stiffeners, a placement keepout of no height; either must span most of the board's width, so a hatched patch on a rigid board is no bend.
- `pcb check` checks a flex board's bend zones, those of `pcb get`: a via in a zone, a trace that turns, changes width or crosses more than 5 degrees off square inside one, and traces of two layers straight above each other across one; and, where the board has stiffeners, a via of a net without plane shapes outside them. It flags a differential pair whose two routed lengths differ by more than 0.127 mm, the pairs recognised by name, such as `USB_DP` and `USB_DN` or `D+` and `D-`; `pcb get` lists each pair's two lengths under `metrics.pairs`. An acute corner's finding says where it is.
- `pcb get` reports the keepouts by kind, with the placement keepouts that limit height, such as a flex board's stiffeners; the cutouts inside the outline; the plane shapes by net, layer and fill, with how many shapes and the extents they cover; and the via padstacks of the design's own library with how many vias use each and each one's pad size, also of one no via uses yet. `--output` adds each keepout's, cutout's and plane shape's geometry.
- `trace create` rounds a corner of the routing plan with an arc when the point carries a third value, the arc's radius, tangent to both segments; the check measures along the arc, and a radius too large for the segments beside it is refused.
- The Skill gains a playbook for routing a flex board between two connectors: read the stiffeners, bend zones and pins, set the rules, plan one lane per net, draw, pour with each bend zone hatched, stitch outside the bends and check. Two knowledge entries back it: `layout-flex-bend-and-stiffener` says how a board marks where it bends and where it is stiffened and what each place allows, and `layout-flex-connector-bus` gives common practice for the fan-out, the lanes and the power of such a board.

### Changed

- Built on tool-driver-cli-spec 1.1.0: a live run counts as evidence until the command's registration or the adapter's code changes, and an evaluation result until its question, a command it lists or the Skills and knowledge change, whatever the version number. A long job that ends with `ok: false`, a write or a read, carries the error the call would have returned, in `code`, `message` and `details`; a command it names to run again is the write's first call, never a spent token. A final job's `action`, `resume`, `title` and `knowledge` are in `details`; only a `blocked` job keeps `action` and `knowledge` at the top. `doc save` compares each field with the tolerance of the command whose write left the value, and a `doc undo` record keeps the undone command's tolerances. The Skill handles a failed job as the error it carries.
- `via update` and `doc list` are verified, not stable: in this release's evaluation the Agents reached the same results without them, a via given to the net class before routing and the documents read from `session list`.

### Fixed

- `trace create` joins a trace end that lies on a pad to the pin's origin, and one that lies on a via to the via's centre, before it draws: Layout connects a trace to a pin or a via only at that exact point, and the coordinates the tool reports are rounded, so a plan that ended inside a pad drew copper that stayed unconnected, with every net reported open.
- `via create`, `via update` and `trace create` take a via padstack from the design's own padstack library when no via of the board uses it and the central library cannot be read, as on a copy of a project whose central library is on another machine's drive; they refused such a via as one whose size is not known.
- `doc create` in file mode refuses a template whose folder holds a file another program keeps from being read, such as a project database Designer, Layout, the packager or the iCDB server is working on, with `E_CONFLICT` naming the files, before anything is copied. A copy that still fails part way names the new project file as asked and lists the files it could not copy; it kept the template's file name before.
- `pcb check` keeps the values of a hazard's description when Layout runs in a language other than English, which hands the labels over COM as question marks: `CC1; Pad Part; J1; Unrouted Pin` instead of `???? : CC1 ...`; a label that ends in a number keeps it, `1: SMD Pad`.
- `trace create`, `trace delete` and `via delete` count a board's traces and vias in one pass instead of one pass per net and layer, so the preview and the read-back of a plan on a board of a few hundred traces finish within the call's budget; the read-back ran out of time there and reported the change as not verified.
- `trace create` reads its traces back by their geometry: each net and layer of the plan reads back `plan_drawn`, true when every point along its planned traces lies on a trace of that net and layer. Layout keeps one planned trace as several objects, split at its arcs and elsewhere, so the count it read back before failed a plan that was drawn.
- The routing metrics of `pcb get` and `pcb check` follow a trace's arcs: an arc's centre counted as a corner, so a rounded corner showed as an acute one, and the arc's length as the way through its centre.
- The preview of `trace create` lists the first 20 reasons a plan is refused and counts the rest; a plan refused for more reasons failed with a response too long, which asked for fewer objects although the plan named few.
- `trace create` checks the two traces of a differential pair against the spacing their class sets for the pair, not against the clearance between nets: a pair drawn at its spacing was refused.
- `trace create` and `via create` follow the board's existing traces along their arcs, within 2 micrometres: an arc's centre was taken as a corner of the trace, so a stitching via could be placed on an arc and a plan drawn across one. The plan's own clearances hold within a micrometre, as concentric arcs checked as chords come that much closer than their copper.
- `trace create` moves a trace end that lies a few nanometres off a pin's origin or a via's centre onto it, instead of adding a segment, and leaves a corner that turns by a few microradians sharp: Layout kept each such sliver as a trace of its own, dozens on a board, which DRC reported as loops and hangers.
- `schematic export` and `bom export` try replacing the file again for two seconds when Windows refuses it, as a virus scanner or the search indexer holding a file it has just seen written does for a moment; such a run failed.
- `doc save` of a schematic reports it saved while Layout has the project's board open. Layout keeps Xpedition's database server running, so closing the project in Designer writes nothing and the save was reported as not reaching the disk; the tool now also looks after opening the project again, when the server writes it.
- `via create` records the padstack of the net's vias when it changes, as `via delete` does when the last via goes, so a later `doc save` no longer reports a mismatch after all of a net's vias were deleted and placed again.

## [1.2.0] - 2026-11-08

### Added

- `component list`, `component get`, `net list` and `net get` read the schematic Designer has open: each component's part number, value, sheet and attributes, each pin with its name and net, and each net with the component pins it joins and the sheets it reaches. An unnamed net goes by the name Designer gives it, such as `$1N54`; a reference designator two components share is refused as ambiguous, with both listed. On a board Layout has open, `component list` and `component get` give each component's part number, cell, position, rotation, side and anchor, and `component get` each pin's net and position; a name that matches two components only in case is refused as ambiguous.
- `component move` moves one component on the open board to a position, rotation and side, or places one not yet placed; a part sent to the bottom is placed there mirrored. While it moves the part, Layout's placement check is set to refuse a spot another part takes instead of shoving that part aside, so no other part moves: such a spot is refused with `E_CONFLICT`. A fixed or locked part is refused at the preview, a semi-fixed part stays semi-fixed when it changes side, and a part whose side Layout does not give moves only with `--side`. Its result says whether the part lies inside the board outline.
- `mounting-hole create` places mounting holes of a mounting-hole padstack of the central library at one spot, or one in each corner of the board outline with `--corners` and `--inset`, taking the padstack from the library when the board lacks it; a hole whose drill or pad would reach past the outline, a rounded corner's arc included, is refused, and so is an inset that would make the corner holes meet, and a spot that holds a hole of another padstack blocks the call. `mounting-hole list` lists the board's own mounting holes, and `mounting-hole delete` deletes the one that holds a point, or all of them; a fixed or locked hole is refused, and the holes of a part's cell stay.
- `refdes move` moves a component's reference designator on the silkscreen to a position and rotation, or with `--auto` to the first spot beside the part, above, below, right or left, clear of the parts, the other reference designators and the mounting holes, inside the outline. A fixed or locked text is refused at the preview. Its result lists what the text now overlaps, its own part included.
- `bom check` checks the schematic's bill of materials: components without a part number, reference designators used twice, part numbers the central library does not hold, and one part number with several values. When the central library cannot be read, such as one on a drive this machine does not have, it checks the rest and says why the library was left out.
- `bom export` writes the schematic's bill of materials to a CSV file Excel reads, one row per part number and value with the central library's description, or the component's own when the library has none or cannot be read, or one row per component with `--per-component`. With `--baseline` it compares the BOM with an earlier one, from this tool or another, its reference designators separated by commas, full-width commas, ideographic enumeration commas or spaces and ranges written `R1-R4`, `R1~R4` or `R1 - R4`, and lists the components added, removed and changed in part number or value. A file another program holds open, as Excel holds a CSV it shows, is refused before anything is written.
- Evaluation questions for these commands and for `trace create`: read a schematic's connections, values and part numbers; check the BOM and compare it with an earlier one; tidy a placed board by moving a part to the bottom, putting a mounting hole in each corner, taking one back and giving each reference designator a free spot; and draw one trace between two named pins, as wide as its class allows, around the other nets' pads.

## [1.1.0] - 2026-11-08

### Added

- `constraint get` reads the board's constraints: net classes and their nets, trace widths and differential spacing per layer, differential pairs, clearance rules and the class pairs that use them; `--output` writes them all in the rules file format.
- `constraint import` creates or changes the net classes, widths, differential spacing, differential pairs and clearance rules a rules file names, writes them into the project database and loads them into Layout; what the file does not name stays. It changes the board's side of the constraints only, is refused while that side holds changes Layout has not loaded, and its receipt says persistence saved, so the board does not count it among its unsaved changes. A width outside its class's minimum or maximum moves the limit the file leaves out to that width.
- The knowledge entry `layout-impedance-from-fab-table` says impedance widths and spacing come from the fabricator's table: Layout's own impedance figures were several hundred ohms for any width on both boards measured.
- `padstack list` lists the padstacks of the central library with their type and their pad, hole and soldermask sizes; `--type via` lists the vias.
- `padstack create` adds a via padstack to the central library: a plated hole, a round pad on every conductor layer, and a soldermask opening with `--mask` or none (tented). A via the library holds as it is is skipped; a name it holds with other content is refused.
- `trace create` draws the traces and places the vias of a routing plan, a JSON file in board coordinates. It first checks the plan offline against the board's copper, the width limits of each net's class and the clearance rules for each pair of classes, and is refused with every reason it finds; its result says which of the plan's nets are routed now. A via the board already uses takes its size from the board's own vias, so a plan works on a project whose central library is on another machine's drive.
- `trace delete` deletes the traces of the nets named, on every layer or on the layers named; `via delete` deletes their vias; `plane delete` deletes the plane shapes of one net on one layer. They take back a routing attempt before a save, and the save's read-back expects neither the padstack of the deleted vias nor the fill of the deleted plane.
- `via update` changes the vias of the nets or net classes named to another via padstack, pulled from the central library when the board lacks it. The autorouter places the via each net class is assigned in Constraint Manager, which no command can change, so a router run followed by via update gives the via size the design asks for.
- `via create` places vias of one net: one at `--x`, `--y`, or a stitching grid at `--pitch` inside the net's copper on two layers, clear of every pad, the traces of other nets, vias and holes by the board's default clearance rule, and 0.254 mm from the board's cutouts, the distance Layout's DRC asks. The via is the net class's own unless `--padstack` names another; a via the board already uses takes its size from the board's own vias.
- `solder-mask create` opens the soldermask over a rectangle on one side, or the coverlay of a flex board with `--cover-layer`, naming the nets whose copper it bares; an opening over no copper, or a coverlay opening on a stackup without a cover layer, is refused. `solder-mask delete` deletes the opening that holds a point.
- `pcb render` draws the board as a PNG from its geometry: outline, pads, traces, vias, plane shapes, holes, cutouts such as slots for screws, and soldermask openings, from the top or the bottom, for chosen layers. Its result counts the pads, traces and plane shapes on the layers drawn and the openings on the side drawn. It needs no screen, so it works with the desktop locked, and runs as a long job, since reading a real board's geometry takes tens of seconds.
- Evaluation questions for the routing commands, on the sample board: put the power nets in a net class of their own width, route only that class and give its vias the size asked for; pour a second GND plane, stitch the two, bare a patch of copper and draw the board; take back the traces, vias, a plane and a soldermask opening; read a net class's width and the library's vias.

### Changed

- The knowledge entries describe what xpedition-cli does now, without version labels; the licence entry says a missing licence call is reported as `E_HUMAN_REQUIRED` with the action `activate_license`, and the Designer prompts entry says doc open leaves the question about closing open documents to the person.
- The knowledge on vias near pads, trace widths, pouring after routing and the board flow names the new commands: trace create and via create place single traces and vias, plane delete takes back a pour, and constraint import sets the rules before any copper.

A person grants live-run targets once, in `docs/E2E.md`; inside them the coding Agent works without asking again.
